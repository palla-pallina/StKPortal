**First Communion**
First Holy Communion is a special Catholic sacrament where children receive the Eucharist—the Body and Blood of Christ—for the first time. A child baptized as an infant into the family of God now takes place at the Lord’s table. This is a momentous event for the child, the family, and the parish community.

The First Communion ceremony takes place during Catholic Mass, which can last anywhere from 60 to 90 minutes total. The priest blesses those about to receive their First Communion and then begins the Eucharist ceremony, during which children receive Holy Communion - the body and blood of Jesus Christ - for the first time, accompanied by their godparents and other family members.

#### Anointing of the Sick
The Sacrament of the Anointing of the Sick is the Church’s sacrament for those who are sick and aging, and is a sacred moment when we pray for God’s grace to strengthen someone who is suffering from any form of illness.  An anointing Mass is generally offered during the seasons of Lent and Easter. Arrangements can be made for a priest to come to your home if you are home bound or too ill to come to daily Mass.

**When Should A Person be Anointed?**

The Rite of Anointing indicates there is no need to wait until a person is at the point of death to receive the Sacrament. A careful judgment about the serious nature of the illness is sufficient. The Sacrament may be repeated if the sick person recovers after the anointing but becomes ill once again, or if, during the same illness, the person’s condition becomes more serious. A person should be anointed before surgery when a dangerous illness is the reason for the intervention.

Parishioners who are seriously ill in any way should request the Sacrament of Anointing.  The healing ministry of the Church follows that of Jesus and is concerned with the healing of the whole person— the body, mind, and spirit. Sometimes a person is cured of a bodily illness. Sometimes there is psychological or spiritual healing. What is certain is that the healing taking place strengthens a sick person and helps them to bear illness.

#### Matrimony

1. **What is Matrimony?**
Marriage is an intimate sharing of life and love. There are four words to describe Marriage:
- **Sacrament**  You do not receive the sacrament of matrimony. It is something you become. You are sacrament in the presence of God’s life and love to each other.
- **Covenant** Marriage is a special kind of relationship. Your relationship is permanent and faithful, in good times and bad times.
- **Commitment** You freely choose to take each other on your wedding day and every day of your life together.
- **Vocation**  God has called you and your spouse at this time of your lives to a mutual self-giving. Through this unselfish love, the world can see Christ’s love for His people enfleshed in your love for each other.

###### Documents
- PSA Birth Certificate
- Baptismal Certificate
- Confirmation Certificate
- Certificate of No Marriage (CENOMAR)
- Government-issued Marriage License
- Affidavit of Parental Consent - for couples below 25yo
- Community Tax Certificate or Barangay Clearance - for proof of residency

###### Interview/Seminar
- ==Canonical Interview== - At least one to two months before the wedding, the church will schedule a canonical interview with the parish priest or his assistant. This meeting helps assess your readiness to enter the sacrament of marriage.
- ==Pre-Cana Seminar== - A marriage preparation seminar **organized by the church**, covering topics like communication, spirituality, family planning, and values. This seminar is a prerequisite for many parishes.

###### Church
- Wedding Banns - This is a public announcement of your wedding, to be posted for three consecutive Sundays at both of your respective parishes. Some churches may require you to submit ID pictures as part of this process. Once the banns are posted, a signed copy will be returned to your wedding church as confirmation.
- Sacrament of Reconciliation (Confession) - Many churches recommend couples go to confession before their wedding, as a way to spiritually prepare for receiving the Sacraments of Matrimony and the Eucharist. ==This can be done a week before the ceremony or on the day before the wedding starts.==

5. **What is the process?**

###### Process of a Church Wedding
1. Submit documents to the parish office at least 3 to 6 months before the wedding date.
2. Attend the Pre-Cana seminar as a couple.
3. Secure the marriage license from the Local Civil Registry.
4. Schedule the wedding date with the parish and pay any reservation fees.
5. Prepare additional documents required by the parish.
6. The wedding ceremony includes the exchange of vows, ring ceremony, and the signing of the marriage contract.
7. The priest submits the signed marriage contract to the LCR within 15 days for registration.
